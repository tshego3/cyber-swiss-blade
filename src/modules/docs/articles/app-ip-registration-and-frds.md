# App IP registration and FRDs

This article shows how to register an application idea as intellectual property (IP) in South Africa. It also shows how to prepare a Functional Requirements Document (FRD) for the app development.

> **IMPORTANT:** Get advice from an IP attorney about the specific requirements and processes for your IP and your application idea in South Africa.

## Register the idea as intellectual property

1. **Do a prior art search.** Make sure that your idea is unique and that no other person has a patent or other protection for it. Search for similar ideas and patents.
2. **Document your idea.** Write a detailed description of the application idea: its features, its functionality, and its unique aspects. Be as specific as possible, so that the description clearly defines your invention.
3. **Use non-disclosure agreements (NDAs).** Before you share the idea with developers, investors, or partners, ask them to sign an NDA. An NDA is a legal obligation for the parties to keep the information confidential.
4. **File a provisional patent application.** File it with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). This gives you an early filing date and secures your priority rights. Include a full description of the invention and the related drawings or diagrams.
5. **Engage an IP attorney.** Use an attorney who specializes in patent law. The attorney can help you prepare the documents and tell you the best approach to protect your IP.
6. **Convert the provisional application to a complete patent application.** Do this in 12 months or less after you file the provisional application. Include all the technical details, the claims, and the other necessary documents. Work closely with your IP attorney to meet all requirements.
7. **Complete the patent examination.** The CIPC examines the complete application for novelty, inventiveness, and industrial applicability. You may have to reply to office actions and make amendments or clarifications to the application.

> **NOTE:** Verify these points with your IP attorney. South Africa has mostly been a non-examining (depository) patent system, where the CIPC checks the formal requirements and does not always examine novelty and inventiveness. Also, the Patents Act excludes a computer program "as such" from patents, so an app idea alone is usually not patentable. Copyright protects the source code automatically.

## Prepare the FRD and the development

1. **Write the FRD.** You can start the FRD while the patent application is in progress. The FRD gives the detailed functional specifications, the user interface, the features, and the interactions of the app. It helps developers understand your requirements, so that they make the app that you want.
2. **Hire a development team.** When the FRD is ready, look for an app development team or company with a good reputation. Examine their portfolios, experience, expertise, and reputation before you decide.
3. **Protect trade secrets.** In addition to patents, protect the app as a trade secret:
   - Limit the access to the source code.
   - Sign confidentiality agreements with the developers.
   - Keep strict control of the app distribution and use.
